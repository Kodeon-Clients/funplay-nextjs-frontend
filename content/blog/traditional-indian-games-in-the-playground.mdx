Ask a grandparent in India what they played as a child and you will hear about gilli-danda, langdi, fugdi, chausar, kite flying on the terrace and rides at the village mela. Many of these games build exactly the skills modern playgrounds aim for: balance, coordination, strategy and cooperation. They are also part of a shared culture children rarely meet in a standard playground.

## Games that inspire equipment

### Stapu

Hopscotch by another name (langdi or kith-kith in some regions), drawn with chalk or a stick in every lane. In a playground it becomes a grid built into a poured rubber surface, or a trail of hopping posts at different heights. See [coloured playground paths and zones](/blog/coloured-playground-paths-and-zones-ideas.html).

### Unt sawari

The camel ride: a highlight of fairs and beaches. A spring rider in the shape of a camel brings that ride into the playground for toddlers. Our [Unt Savari spring rider](/funplay-originals/organic-play/unt-savari.html) is one example.

### Fugdi

Two children hold hands and spin, faster and faster. Spinning poles and rotating equipment capture the same feeling. Our Fugadi revolving pole is named after it.

### Chausar and paheli

Board games of strategy and puzzles become play structures and panels where children climb, count and solve together.

### Patang

Kite flying is a festival in itself. Our Patang kite swing set turns the kite into a swing frame.

### Dahi handi

The human pyramid of Janmashtami becomes a figure climbing wall, our Dahi Handi climber.

## Why cultural play works

- **Familiarity:** children see games their parents and grandparents talk about.
- **Conversation:** grandparents explain the game, which brings generations together in the playground.
- **Identity:** the play area feels local, not imported.
- **Skills:** balance, rhythm, cooperation and strategy, the same as any good playground.

## Where it fits

- **Schools:** culture and play together; useful for play-based learning. See [how play-based learning can transform education in India](/blog/how-play-based-learning-can-transform-education-in-india.html).
- **Parks:** a distinctive landmark for the neighbourhood.
- **Resorts and heritage properties:** play that suits a local theme.
