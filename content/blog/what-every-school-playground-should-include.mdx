Ask children what makes a good playground and they will name a slide, a swing and a place to run. Ask teachers and they will add shade, safety and a spot where they can see everyone. A good school playground needs all of these. Here is a checklist, with a short note on why each item matters.

## 1. Something for every kind of play

Children need different kinds of movement for different skills. Aim to cover each one:

## 2. Zones by age

Pre-primary, primary and older children play differently and at different speeds. Give them separate areas. See [playground equipment for primary and pre-primary schools](/blog/playground-equipment-for-primary-and-pre-primary-schools.html).

## 3. Open ground

Tag, kabaddi, kho-kho, skipping, ball games and cycling need open space, not structures. Keep a clear area next to the equipment so running games do not happen on the play towers.

## 4. Safe surfaces

Under all equipment and across its safety zones: rubber, poured rubber, turf with a shock pad, or deep sand. Compare them in [school playground surface materials](/blog/school-playground-surface-materials-which-one-is-best.html).

## 5. Shade, water and seating

Trees, shade sails or covered seating make the playground usable in April and May. Drinking water nearby keeps children from leaving the area. Seating for teachers should face the equipment.

## 6. Clear sight lines and boundaries

Staff on duty should see the whole area from one spot. Fence the playground from gates, parking and driveways.

## 7. Room for everyone

Include ground-level play, wide platforms and equipment several children can use together, so children with different abilities can join in. See [the growing need for inclusive playgrounds](/blog/growing-need-for-inclusive-playgrounds-in-indian-communities.html).

## 8. A maintenance routine

Name a person responsible and give them a daily and monthly checklist. See our [playground safety inspection checklist](/blog/playground-safety-inspection-checklist.html).
