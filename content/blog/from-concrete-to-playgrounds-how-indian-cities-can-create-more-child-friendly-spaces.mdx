Most Indian cities were not planned around children. Lanes became roads, open plots became buildings, and the play spaces left are often far away or poorly kept. But cities do not need vast new parks to become more child-friendly. Many small changes, close to where children live, add up.

## Many small spaces, close to home

A child under eight will not travel far to play, and parents will not take them far on a weekday. A network of small play spaces within walking distance of homes reaches more children than one large park across town.

## Where play can go

- **Unused plots and corners** turned into pocket parks.
- **Rooftops and podiums** in residential and commercial buildings.
- **School grounds** opened to the neighbourhood after hours.
- **Streets** closed to traffic at set times for play.
- **Everyday routes:** play elements near bus stops, markets, clinics and schools.
- **Under flyovers and along waterfronts**, with careful design for safety.

## Who can act

- **City authorities:** plan play into parks, streets and redevelopment, and fund maintenance.
- **Builders:** plan proper play areas in every project. See [playground amenities for real estate projects](/blog/playground-amenities-for-real-estate-projects.html).
- **Housing societies:** make better use of podiums, terraces and corners. See [how to plan a society play area](/blog/how-to-plan-a-society-play-area.html).
- **Schools:** share grounds and design playgrounds that serve the community.
- **Companies:** fund play spaces through CSR in under-served areas.

## Make them last

Every new space needs durable equipment, safe surfaces and someone responsible for upkeep. See [challenges in building playgrounds in Indian cities](/blog/challenges-in-building-playgrounds-in-indian-cities.html).

## Quick wins for a neighbourhood

- **Restore a neglected park:** repair or replace broken equipment, add safety surfacing and lighting.
- **Open a school ground** on evenings and weekends with a simple agreement.
- **Add play to a plaza or market** with a few robust items and seating.
- **Hold a play street** on a Sunday morning to show what is possible.
- **Turn a society corner** into a toddler play area.

## Designing small play spaces well

Small spaces need careful design: compact structures, play panels on walls, games in the floor and clear safety zones. See [playground design ideas for small spaces](/blog/playground-design-ideas-for-small-spaces.html) and [terrace and podium play areas](/blog/terrace-and-podium-play-areas-for-high-rise-buildings.html).
