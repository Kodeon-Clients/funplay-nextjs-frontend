Playground catalogues are full of names that mean little outside the industry: play stations, spinners, spring riders, tepee towers. This guide explains the main types of playground equipment, what each one does for children, which ages it suits, and where it works best.

## Multi play stations

A **multi play station** combines platforms, steps, bridges, climbers and slides in one structure. It is the centrepiece of most school and society playgrounds because many children can use it at once in a compact footprint. They range from small toddler units to large structures with several towers, tube slides and climbing nets.

- **Ages:** from 2 to 12 depending on the platform heights.
- **Best for:** schools, societies, parks and resorts.
- **Read more:** [what is a multi play station?](/blog/what-is-a-multi-play-station.html)

## Slides

Every child's favourite. Slides are sold as standalone units or as part of a play station, and are described by the height of the platform they start from (3 ft, 5 ft and 7 ft are common sizes). Types include straight, wave, curve, spiral and tube slides.

- **Ages:** 3 ft for toddlers, 5 ft for primary children, 7 ft and above for older children.
- **Plan for:** a clear run-out at the exit. Compare materials in [playground slide materials compared](/blog/playground-slide-materials-compared.html).

## Swings

Swings build balance and rhythm, and are one of the few items children of every age use. Types include standard belt-seat swings, toddler swings with enclosed bucket seats, nest swings that hold several children, and mother-and-child swings.

- **Plan for:** a large clear zone in front of and behind the seats, at the edge of the play area. See [types of playground swings](/blog/types-of-playground-swings.html).

## Climbers and climbing nets

Climbers build strength, grip and confidence. They include climbing walls, rope and net climbers, dome climbers, monkey bars, ladders and rotating net towers. Many can be used by several children at once.

- **Ages:** low climbers from 3 years; tall nets for 6 and above.
- **Plan for:** the right surface for the fall height.

## See-saws

See-saws teach cooperation: they only work with a partner. Single, double and multi-seat versions exist, plus spring see-saws for younger children.

## Merry-go-rounds and spinners

Spinning equipment builds balance and is a big draw in parks and schools. Options include seated merry-go-rounds, standing platforms, cage spinners and single-child spinners.

- **Plan for:** speed limiting and a level, safe surface around the edge.

## Spring riders

Animal, vehicle and character riders on a heavy spring. They are ideal for toddlers and for filling gaps in a layout.

## Balance equipment, tunnels and stepping posts

Balancing beams, wobbly bridges, crawl tunnels and hopping posts are low, cheap and add a lot of play value at ground level. They also let children who do not want to climb join in.

## Play panels and pretend play

Panels with games, puzzles, music elements, mirrors and counting frames, and themed structures such as houses, ships and shops. They encourage talking, turn-taking and imagination, and are essential in pre-primary areas.

## Sand and water play

Sand tables, diggers and water channels. They need maintenance, but children play with them longer than almost anything else.

## Outdoor gym equipment

Fitness machines for teenagers and adults: walkers, cross trainers, leg presses, twisters, pull-up stations. Often placed next to a children's play area in parks and societies so parents can exercise while they watch. See our [outdoor gym equipment list](/blog/outdoor-gym-equipment-list.html).

## Site furniture

Benches, gazebos, pergolas and bins turn a set of equipment into a place people want to spend time in.
