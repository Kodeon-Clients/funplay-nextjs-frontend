A park or society garden is valuable precisely because it is green. Adding a playground should not mean replacing lawns and trees with one large rubber pad. With some care, play equipment can sit inside a green space and make it more used, not less.

## Cluster, don't pave

Instead of one large play area, place equipment in smaller clusters: a toddler corner under a tree, a swing set at the lawn edge, a climbing structure on a gentle rise. Paths link them through the planting. Only the safety zones need impact surfaces; the rest stays green.

## Work with the trees

Trees give shade and character, but their roots are easily damaged:

- Keep foundations and excavation out of the root zone of mature trees.
- Use surfaces that let water through near trees.
- Check for dead or brittle branches overhead.
- Avoid planting thorny, toxic or heavy-fruiting trees near play.

## Choose colours and materials that belong

Bright primary colours suit some settings, but in a garden, earthy reds, greens, browns and natural wood finishes look at home. ## Surfaces that look green

Artificial turf over a tested shock pad gives impact protection while staying green. Poured rubber in green or brown tones is another option. See [fall height and safety surfacing](/blog/playground-fall-height-and-safety-surfacing-guide.html).

## Plant around the play

Low planting between clusters guides children along paths and softens edges. Choose hardy, non-toxic plants without thorns, and keep them out of safety zones.

## A simple planning sequence

1. Map trees, lawns, slopes and drainage.
2. Mark root zones and areas to keep green.
3. Choose equipment clusters and place them in open areas.
4. Draw safety zones and surfacing for each cluster.
5. Link clusters with paths through planting.
6. Add seating under trees with a view of the play.

See [playground layout and safety zones](/blog/playground-layout-and-safety-zones.html) for the layout method.
