The slide is the most popular item in almost every playground. It is also the part that takes the most wear and sits in full sun. The material it is made from affects how long it lasts, how fast it is, how hot it gets and how it looks after a few years.

## The three common materials

### Roto-moulded plastic

Most slides in Indian playgrounds are roto-moulded polyethylene: a seamless, hollow part with rounded edges and a smooth bed. It comes in bright colours and can be moulded into straight, wave, curve, spiral and tube shapes. UV stabilisers slow fading. It warms up in the sun but less than metal, and lighter colours help.

### Stainless steel

Steel slides are extremely durable and resistant to vandalism, and many children find them faster. The big drawback in India is heat: in direct sun a steel slide can become hot enough to burn. They work best in shade, under trees or facing north.

### FRP (fibreglass)

Fibreglass slides are rigid and can be repaired, and are often used for large custom shapes. Over time the gelcoat surface can chalk and fade, and impact can cause cracks. Read more in [roto-moulded plastic vs FRP](/blog/roto-moulded-plastic-vs-frp-play-equipment.html).

## Choosing the shape

- **Straight and wave slides** are the simplest and suit all ages.
- **Curve and spiral slides** fit a long ride into a smaller footprint.
- **Tube slides** are a favourite with older children and stay shaded inside, but staff cannot see inside them.

## Safety around slides

- A clear run-out at the exit, on an impact-absorbing surface.
- No benches, walls or other equipment in front of the exit.
- Touch the bed on hot afternoons before small children slide.
- Check for cracks and loose joints monthly.
