A play area that clashes with its surroundings looks like an afterthought. One that shares the landscape's colours, materials and forms looks designed. Here are ideas for five common landscape styles.

## Tropical and resort gardens

Lush planting, palms and lawns welcome playful, nature-inspired designs: play houses that look like trees, animal spring riders, climbing nets among the palms.

## Contemporary landscapes

Clean lines and minimal planting suit abstract, sculptural structures in a limited palette. Our [NexLev Abstract](/nexlev-series/abstract.html) structures are designed for these settings.

## Nature-led and eco landscapes

Organic shapes, natural textures and earthy colours. Combine equipment with logs, boulders and planting. See [using natural elements with play equipment](/blog/using-natural-elements-with-play-equipment-for-immersive-spaces.html).

## Formal and heritage settings

Castle forms, wood finishes and muted colours look at home beside heritage architecture or formal lawns.

## Urban podiums

Neutral hardscape lets one bright, compact play structure become the focal point.

## Matching by project type

- **Resorts:** play that suits the resort's character: tropical, heritage, hill or beach. See [kids' play areas for resorts and hotels](/blog/kids-play-area-for-resorts-and-hotels.html).
- **Premium residential:** equipment that picks up the colours and materials of the facade and landscaping.
- **Public parks:** robust, colourful equipment that reads clearly from a distance and suits the park's planting.
- **Schools:** bright, durable equipment, perhaps with a nature or cultural theme linked to the school's identity.

## Practical tips for designers

1. **Collect the palette** of the landscape: paving, planting, furniture, buildings.
2. **Choose one hero structure** and let other equipment support it.
3. **Limit colours** to two or three main ones plus neutrals.
4. **Check the safe play area** of each unit early, so the layout works with paths and planting. See [playground layout and safety zones](/blog/playground-layout-and-safety-zones.html).
5. **Think about the surface** colour too: it covers more area than the equipment.

## When the landscape is not yet built

On new projects, the play area is often designed before planting matures. Choose equipment that looks right with young planting as well as mature trees, and plan shade structures until trees grow. See [playground shade ideas](/blog/playground-shade-ideas-for-indian-summers.html).
