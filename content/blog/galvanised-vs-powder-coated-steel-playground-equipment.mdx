Steel is the skeleton of almost every playground and outdoor gym. Left unprotected, steel rusts quickly in Indian rain and humidity, and faster still near the sea. How it is protected is one of the most important details in any specification, and one of the easiest to overlook.

## Three ways to protect steel

### Paint

Liquid paint is the cheapest finish. It looks fine when new, but every chip and scratch exposes the steel, and rust spreads underneath the paint from there. On outdoor play equipment, painted-only steel usually shows rust within a few monsoons.

### Powder coating

Powder coating applies dry coloured powder that is baked into a hard, even layer. It is thicker and tougher than paint and comes in bright colours. But it is still only a barrier: if it is chipped, water reaches the steel and rust creeps under the coating.

### Galvanising

Galvanising coats the steel in zinc, usually by dipping it in molten zinc. Zinc protects steel in two ways: as a barrier, and by corroding in place of the steel where the surface is scratched. That second effect is why galvanised steel keeps protecting itself after small damage.

## Why the best equipment uses both

Galvanising then powder coating, often called a duplex system, combines the self-protecting zinc layer with a tough, coloured outer skin. Together they last much longer than either alone. ## Where rust starts, and how to stop it

- **The base of posts,** where water and dirt collect. Keep surfacing and soil from piling against posts.
- **Welds and joints,** where coatings are thinnest.
- **Drilled holes and cut ends,** if they were made after coating.
- **Contact points,** such as swing hangers and handles, worn by use.

Check these spots every month. Clean any rust spots, prime and touch up with a matching coating, and report anything deeper than the surface to your supplier. See our [playground safety inspection checklist](/blog/playground-safety-inspection-checklist.html).
