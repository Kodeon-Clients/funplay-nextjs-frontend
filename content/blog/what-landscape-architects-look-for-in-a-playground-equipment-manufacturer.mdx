For landscape architects, the play area is one element in a larger design: levels, planting, paths, drainage and views all have to work together. A playground manufacturer who understands that is a far easier partner than one who only sends a catalogue. Here is what architects tell us they look for.

## 1. Technical information, early

To place equipment in a scheme, an architect needs:

- Plan and elevation drawings, ideally in CAD.
- Footprint and **safety zone** dimensions.
- **Free fall height**, age range and user capacity.
- Foundation details and base plate options for podiums.
- Material and finish specifications.
- Recommended **surfacing** and its build-up depth for each unit.

Without this, safety zones end up overlapping planting beds or paths, and surfacing levels clash with hardscape.

## 2. Design that suits the landscape

Bright primary colours suit some sites; others need natural tones, timber or forms inspired by nature. Architects look for ranges that offer:

- Colour options, including muted and earthy palettes.
- Timber and natural-looking materials.
- Nature-inspired and sculptural designs.
- Willingness to adapt a design to a site.

## 3. Safety information they can stand behind

Architects carry responsibility for the scheme. They need to know which standard each item is designed to, what the safety zones are, and what surface is needed. See [playground safety standards in India](/blog/playground-safety-standards-in-india.html).

## 4. Coordination on site

The playground installer and the landscape contractor have to agree on finished levels, surfacing build-up, drainage, edging and sequence. A manufacturer who joins site meetings and adapts to the programme saves everyone time.

## 5. Reliable delivery

A late or poorly installed play area reflects on the whole project. Architects look for manufacturers with their own installation teams, realistic timelines and projects they can visit.
