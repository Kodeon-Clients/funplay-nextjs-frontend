The best play spaces feel as if they grew out of the landscape. That takes coordination between the landscape architect, the playground supplier and the contractor, mostly on details that are easy to miss: levels, drainage, edges and roots.

## Use the landform

Flat sites are easy, but slopes and mounds are more fun:

- **Slides down a bank** need less structure and look natural.
- **Climbing walls and nets** can face a slope.
- **Lookouts and play houses** sit well on a rise.
- **Tunnels** can pass through a mound.

Retain and drain slopes properly, and keep the bottom of every slide and wall clear for its run-out and safety zone.

## Agree levels and build-up early

The surface under equipment has a build-up depth (base plus rubber or shock pad and turf). It must meet surrounding paths and lawns flush, without trip edges. Agree finished levels at design stage and show them on drawings.

## Drainage

Play surfaces should fall towards drains, not towards planting beds or tree pits. Where turf meets planting, prevent soil and mulch from washing onto the play surface in heavy rain.

## Trees and roots

Keep foundations and excavation out of the root zone of mature trees. Where a tree is part of the design, keep its trunk and low branches out of safety zones and check it regularly.

## Planting

- Thornless, non-toxic species.
- Outside safety zones, with branches trimmed clear of platforms.
- Hardy plants that survive being run past by children.

## Edges

Where rubber meets turf, or turf meets planting, use edging that holds each material in place and leaves no step.

## Plants to choose and avoid near play areas

- **Choose:** hardy, non-toxic, thornless shrubs and grasses; shade trees with strong branches; plants that tolerate being brushed past.
- **Avoid:** thorny plants, species with toxic leaves or berries, trees with brittle branches or heavy fruit fall, and plants that drop slippery leaves on play surfaces.

Your landscape architect will know the local species; share the play layout with them so planting stays outside safety zones.
