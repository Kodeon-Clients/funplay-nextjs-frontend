Outdoor gym machines are designed to be simple: no weights to set, no cables, no electricity. They use the user's own body weight for resistance, so a beginner and a regular can use the same machine at their own pace. Here is what you will find in a typical open gym.

## Cardio machines

- **Walker (air walker).** Legs swing back and forth on pendulum arms. Low impact and the most popular machine in most parks.
- **Cross trainer.** Combined arm and leg movement, like an elliptical trainer. Works the whole body.
- **Cycle.** A seated pedal machine, good for knees and for older users.
- **Rower.** A seated pull with the arms and push with the legs.
- **Step trainer.** A stair-climbing movement for legs and hips.
- **Ski trainer.** Side-to-side leg movement with arm poles.

## Lower body strength

- **Leg press.** Push away from a footplate, lifting your own body weight.
- **Leg extension.** Strengthens the thigh muscles.
- **Leg lift post.** Raised-leg exercises for core and hips.

## Upper body strength

- **Chest press.** Push handles forward while seated.
- **Shoulder press.** Push handles upwards while seated.
- **Pull-up station.** Bars at different heights for pull-ups and hanging.
- **Parallel bars.** Dips and supported leg raises.

## Flexibility, mobility and balance

- **Twister (seated or standing).** Rotating discs that loosen the waist and back.
- **Tai chi wheel.** Large hand wheels turned in circles for shoulder mobility.
- **Shoulder twirl.** A gentle rotating handle for the shoulders.
- **Pendulum.** Swinging side to side for the hips and core.
- **Ab trainer.** For sit-ups and core work.

## Combination machines and multi gyms

Many machines combine two or three exercises on one post, such as a leg press with a fun walker and a twister, or a chest press with a shoulder press. Multi gyms put several bar exercises on one frame for groups. They save space and budget.
