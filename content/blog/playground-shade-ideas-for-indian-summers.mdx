For about four months a year, much of India is too hot for an unshaded playground in the afternoon. Metal gets too hot to touch, dark plastic and rubber radiate heat, and children simply stay away. Shade is not a luxury; it decides whether the play area is used at all.

## Where the shade needs to be

The hottest sun comes from the west in the afternoon and from the south for much of the year. Shade on those sides of the play area does the most good. Watch where shadows fall at 3 pm in April before deciding.

## Ways to create shade

### Trees

The best long-term shade, and they cool the air too. Plant on the west and south sides, outside the safety zones, and choose species with no thorns, poisonous fruit or brittle branches. Until they grow, combine them with other shade.

### Shade sails and canopies

Fabric sails over the play area give quick, flexible shade. They need posts and fixings designed for wind and must stay clear of climbing routes.

### Roofs on play structures

Many multi play stations come with roofs over the towers, shading the decks where children gather. Tepee towers and play houses also give shaded spots inside.

### Gazebos and pergolas

Covered seating for parents and teachers, close to the equipment, keeps the adults there too.

## Cooler colours and surfaces

- **Slides:** lighter colours stay cooler than dark ones; metal slides belong in shade.
- **Rubber surfaces:** very dark colours in full sun get hot. Use mid and light tones where there is no shade.
- **Turf:** can get warm; shade and lighter shades help.

See [design ideas for coloured playground paths and zones](/blog/coloured-playground-paths-and-zones-ideas.html).

## Timing and habits

- Plan outdoor play for mornings and late afternoons in peak summer.
- Touch slides and platforms before small children use them.
- Keep drinking water close.
