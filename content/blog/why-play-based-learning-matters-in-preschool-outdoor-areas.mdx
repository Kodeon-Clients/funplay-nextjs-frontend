For preschool children, there is no clear line between playing and learning. The outdoor area is where much of their learning happens, and its design shapes what they can learn there.

## What preschoolers learn outside

- **Motor skills:** climbing, running, balancing, rocking, digging.
- **Language:** describing, negotiating, storytelling during play.
- **Social skills:** sharing, taking turns, cooperating.
- **Early maths and science:** counting steps, comparing sizes, pouring water, filling and emptying.
- **Confidence:** trying something new and succeeding.

## Why outdoors is different

Outdoors children can move with their whole bodies, make noise and mess, explore natural materials and take manageable risks, all of which are harder indoors.

## Designing for learning

- Low play units for climbing and sliding.
- Spring riders for rocking and balance.
- Sand and water play.
- Play panels with counting, shapes and mazes.
- A pretend-play house or vehicle.
- Open space for circle games and running.
- Shade and seating for teachers.

## The teacher's role

Set up the space, ask open questions ("How many steps did you climb?"), join in to model language, and observe. Let children lead.

## A morning in a good preschool play area

Picture a group of four-year-olds outside. Two are pushing sand into a bucket, counting scoops as they go. Three are in the play house, deciding who is the shopkeeper. One is climbing the steps of the low play unit, slowly, then sliding down and doing it again, a little faster each time. A teacher crouches by the sand and asks, "Which bucket is heavier?"

Each of those children is learning: counting and comparing, language and turn-taking, balance and confidence. None of it looks like a lesson, which is exactly why it works.

## Common concerns from parents

- **"Shouldn't they be learning to read and write?"** Early literacy grows from language, attention and fine motor control, all of which play builds. Play and early academics support each other.
- **"Isn't outdoor play risky?"** Well-designed preschool equipment is low and enclosed, with safe surfaces. Small, manageable challenges teach children to judge risk.
- **"My child just runs around."** Running, chasing and stopping build coordination, stamina and self-control.

## Linking outdoor play to the curriculum

- **Language:** describe the play, retell what happened, name colours and shapes on panels.
- **Numeracy:** count steps, compare sizes, sort natural objects, time turns.
- **Environmental awareness:** watch plants grow, notice insects and weather.
- **Physical development:** climbing, balancing and throwing are learning goals in themselves.

The National Education Policy 2020 places play- and activity-based learning at the centre of the foundational stage. A well-planned outdoor area is one of the easiest ways for a preschool to put that into practice.
