In almost every school, society and park playground we design, one structure does most of the work: the **multi play station**. Also called a multiplay system or simply a play system, it packs slides, climbers, bridges and lookouts into one footprint so many children can play at once. This guide explains what is in one and how to choose the right one for your site.

## What a multi play station includes

A typical station is built around towers with platforms at one or more heights, connected by:

- **Access:** steps, ladders, ramps, climbing walls or nets.
- **Links:** bridges, tunnels, wobbly or rope bridges.
- **Exits:** straight, wave, spiral or tube slides, fireman's poles.
- **Activity:** play panels, steering wheels, telescopes, counting frames, roofs and themed elements.

Larger stations add swings, climbing frames or monkey bars attached to the structure.

## Why choose one structure instead of separate items

- **More play in less space.** Shared safety zones and stacked activities make better use of a small plot.
- **Social play.** Children move between activities and play together rather than queueing at single items.
- **One focal point.** The play area looks planned, not scattered.
- **Simpler maintenance.** One structure, one foundation layout, one inspection.

## How to choose

### 1. Start with the users

How many children will play at peak time: break time in a school, 6 pm in a society? And how old are they? A primary school with 300 children in one break needs very different equipment from a society of 80 flats.

### 2. Choose the platform height

Platform height decides the age range, the slide size and the surface you need under it.

Mixed-height stations with a low and a high tower can serve a wider range, but put toddlers in their own area if you can. See [designing age-appropriate zones](/blog/designing-age-appropriate-zones-in-outdoor-playgrounds.html).

### 3. Check the space, including safety zones

The footprint in a catalogue is the structure only. Add the impact area around it, typically at least 1.5 m on every side and more at slide exits. Our guide to [playground layout and safety zones](/blog/playground-layout-and-safety-zones.html) explains how.

### 4. Pick a theme

Themes make a play area feel like a place, not a product. On standard designs they usually cost little or nothing extra. Our ranges include castles, jungles, robots, circuses and nature-inspired designs.

### 5. Compare materials, not just pictures

Check the steel (galvanised and powder coated), the plastic (roto-moulded and UV-stabilised), the decks (anti-slip), the roofs and the fixings. Two stations that look alike can last very different lengths of time. See [choosing durable playground materials](/blog/choosing-durable-playground-materials.html).
