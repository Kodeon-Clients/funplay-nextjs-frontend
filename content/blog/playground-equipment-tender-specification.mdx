Many public playgrounds in India start with a tender. The tender decides what gets built far more than any later meeting. A vague specification invites the cheapest bid that technically complies; a clear one makes bids comparable and rewards equipment that will last.

This guide is for procurement teams in municipal corporations, development authorities, PSUs, railways, universities and large schools. It is a starting point, not legal advice: follow your organisation's procurement rules.

## 1. Describe the site and the users

- Location, area, ground conditions, access, drainage, existing features.
- Expected users: age groups and approximate numbers at peak.
- Any requirements for inclusive play.
- A site plan or survey drawing, if available.

## 2. Name the safety standard

State the standard the equipment and surfacing must meet, for example EN 1176 for equipment and EN 1177 for surfacing, and ask for evidence of compliance. See [playground safety standards in India](/blog/playground-safety-standards-in-india.html).

## 3. Specify performance and materials, not a brand

Copying one supplier's catalogue limits competition and often leads to disputes. Instead specify:

## 4. Schedule the equipment

List the equipment by type and function, with age range, user capacity and maximum footprint including safety zones, for example "multi play station for ages 5 to 12, capacity at least 25 children". Allow equivalents.

## 5. Include the full scope

- **Safety surfacing:** type, critical fall height requirement and area (all safety zones).
- **Civil work:** excavation, base, drainage, edging.
- **Transport, installation, testing and commissioning.**
- **Signage:** rules and age boards.
- **Handover:** drawings, manuals, warranty documents.

## 6. Warranty and maintenance

State minimum warranty periods by component, response times for defects, and whether an AMC is included for a number of years. See [warranty and AMC](/blog/playground-equipment-warranty-and-amc.html).

## 8. Evaluate on quality and life-cycle cost

Lowest-price-only evaluation tends to buy equipment that is replaced sooner. Where your rules allow, give weight to technical compliance, play value per user, references and warranty, and compare life-cycle cost including maintenance.
