Good equipment can be made unsafe by poor installation. A post set in too little concrete, a surface laid on loose soil, a swing hung too close to a wall: these are installation problems, not product problems. This guide walks through what happens on a typical playground installation in India, so you know what to expect and what to prepare.

## Step 1: Site survey

Before the layout is final, someone from the installation team should visit the site or review detailed photos and measurements. They check:

- The real dimensions, levels and slopes.
- Soil or slab: natural ground, filled ground, podium or terrace.
- Drainage: where water collects in the monsoon.
- Access for vehicles and materials, and storage space.
- Overhead and underground services: cables, water pipes, waterproofing.
- Trees, walls, fences and anything within the safety zones.

## Step 2: Final layout and approval

The supplier marks every unit, its safety zone and the surfacing on a drawing. The school, society or architect signs off the layout before manufacturing or dispatch. Changes after this point cost time.

## Step 3: Site preparation

Usually the client's or contractor's scope: clearing, levelling, compaction, any base concrete for rubber surfacing, edging and drains. Confirm in the quote who does what. Our [quote guide](/blog/quote-guide-for-playground-equipment-what-to-consider-before-purchasing.html) explains how to check this.

## Step 4: Setting out and foundations

The team marks the exact position of each post on the ground, digs the footings and sets anchor bolts or posts in concrete. On podiums and terraces, base plates are fixed to the slab instead, after checking load capacity and waterproofing; see [terrace and podium play areas](/blog/terrace-and-podium-play-areas-for-high-rise-buildings.html).

**Curing time matters.** Concrete needs time to gain strength before it carries the weight of a structure and children. Loading foundations too early is the most common reason posts later wobble.

## Step 5: Assembly

Structures are assembled, levelled and plumbed, and all fixings are tightened to the manufacturer's specification. Moving parts are checked for free movement and correct clearances. Bolt caps and covers are fitted.

## Step 6: Safety surfacing

Once the equipment is fixed, the surface goes in: rubber tiles on a prepared base, poured rubber in layers, turf on a shock pad, or loose fill to the right depth. It must cover every safety zone at the thickness specified for the fall height. See our [fall height and surfacing guide](/blog/playground-fall-height-and-safety-surfacing-guide.html).

Poured rubber needs dry weather and time to set. In the monsoon, plan around the rain or use tiles.

## Step 7: Final check and handover

Before the play area opens, the installer should:

- Check every unit for stability, fixings, gaps and sharp edges.
- Confirm the surface depth and coverage.
- Remove all debris, packaging and tools.
- Hand over the maintenance guide, warranty terms and a contact for service.
- Walk your maintenance person through the daily and monthly checks.

Only then should children use it.
