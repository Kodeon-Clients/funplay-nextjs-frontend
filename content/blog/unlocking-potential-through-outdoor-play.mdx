Ask adults about their best childhood memories and many involve playing outside: in the lane, the colony ground, the school field. Today many children spend far less time outdoors. That matters, because outdoor play does things for children that little else can.

## Physical benefits

- **Fitness and strength** from running, climbing and hanging.
- **Balance and coordination** from swings, beams and uneven ground.
- **Healthy weight and bones** through regular active play.
- **Sunlight** outdoors in sensible amounts.

The World Health Organization recommends that children aged 5 to 17 get an average of at least 60 minutes a day of moderate to vigorous physical activity. Outdoor play is one of the most natural ways to get there.

## Mental and emotional benefits

- **Focus:** time outdoors and physical activity help many children concentrate better afterwards. See [how physical play improves focus](/blog/how-physical-play-reduces-screen-time-and-improves-focus-in-students.html).
- **Mood:** play relieves stress and frustration.
- **Sleep:** active children tend to sleep better.

## Social and creative benefits

Free play with other children teaches sharing, turn-taking, negotiating rules and resolving arguments. Open-ended spaces, such as nature play areas and themed structures, invite imaginative games.

## How to encourage outdoor play

- **Make time:** a fixed time each day, especially on school days.
- **Make it close:** a safe play area within walking distance makes daily play realistic.
- **Make it interesting:** variety of equipment, open ground, natural elements.
- **Limit screens:** agree screen-time limits as a family.
- **Let children lead:** adults supervise, children choose the game.
- **Invite friends:** children stay out longer together.

For schools, see [educational benefits of outdoor play in schools](/blog/educational-benefits-of-outdoor-play-in-schools.html). For societies, see [creating child-friendly play zones](/blog/creating-child-friendly-play-zones-in-residential-areas.html).

## Outdoor play at different ages

- **Toddlers:** short bursts of crawling, climbing low steps, rocking and sand play, always close to an adult.
- **Preschoolers:** running, sliding, swinging and pretend play with friends.
- **Primary children:** climbing, games with rules, balance challenges and team games.
- **Pre-teens and teenagers:** sport, climbing nets, cycling and time with friends.

Good play areas offer something for each stage. See [designing age-appropriate zones](/blog/designing-age-appropriate-zones-in-outdoor-playgrounds.html).
