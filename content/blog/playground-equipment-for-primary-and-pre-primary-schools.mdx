A playground that works for a 4-year-old is boring for a 10-year-old, and one that thrills a 10-year-old is risky for a 4-year-old. Schools with both pre-primary and primary sections get the best results by planning two playgrounds that share a site, not one that tries to suit everyone.

## What pre-primary children need (ages 3 to 6)

Children at this age are still learning basic movement: climbing steps, balancing, crouching, sliding, rocking. They play side by side and in small groups, often with pretend games. Good pre-primary equipment is:

- **Low:** platforms under about a metre, short slides.
- **Easy to get on and off:** steps and ramps rather than ladders.
- **Enclosed:** side panels and rails on platforms.
- **Varied at ground level:** play panels, tunnels, sand, spring riders.

## What primary children need (ages 6 to 11)

Primary children want challenge: height, speed, spinning, upper-body climbing and games with rules. They play in larger groups and need open ground for running. Good primary equipment includes:

- **A multi play station** with 5 ft slides, climbing walls and bridges.
- **Upper-body challenge:** monkey bars, nets, rotating climbers.
- **Movement:** swings, see-saws, merry-go-rounds or spinners.
- **Balance:** beams, wobbly bridges, stepping posts.

## Separate the zones

- Use a path, low planting or a change in surface colour as the boundary.
- Keep the pre-primary zone closest to the pre-primary classrooms.
- Put the busiest primary equipment furthest from the little ones.
- Give each zone its own seating for teachers.

See [designing age-appropriate zones](/blog/designing-age-appropriate-zones-in-outdoor-playgrounds.html) for layout ideas.

## Surfaces

Both zones need impact-absorbing surfaces under all equipment. Poured rubber suits pre-primary areas well because games, numbers and paths can be built into it. See [school playground surface materials](/blog/school-playground-surface-materials-which-one-is-best.html).
