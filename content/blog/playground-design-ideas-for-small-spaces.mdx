In Indian cities, the space for a play area is often a courtyard between buildings, a strip beside the boundary wall or a corner of a podium. Small does not have to mean dull. With the right choices, a compact play area can be as busy and loved as a big one.

## 1. Go vertical

A compact tower with a climbing net, a deck and a slide stacks several activities into one footprint. Tepee towers and play houses do the same with a strong visual identity. Look for designs with a high user capacity relative to their footprint.

## 2. Use the walls

Boundary and building walls can carry play panels: mazes, counting frames, mirrors, music elements and climbing holds at low height. They add play without using floor space. See our [Wall Play range](/funplay-originals/wall-play.html).

## 3. Use the floor

Poured rubber can carry hopscotch, number tracks, mazes and racing lanes, turning open space into play space. See [coloured playground paths and zones](/blog/coloured-playground-paths-and-zones-ideas.html).

## 4. Pick equipment for groups

Nest swings, rotating net climbers and multi-seat see-saws let several children play at once, reducing queues in a small area.

## 5. Plan safety zones first

In a small space it is tempting to squeeze things together. Do the opposite: mark the safe area of each unit first, then choose the equipment that fits. See [playground layout and safety zones](/blog/playground-layout-and-safety-zones.html).

## 6. Keep a little open space

Even a small open patch for running, circle games or a ball game makes the play area work for more children.

## 7. Think about podiums and terraces

Elevated spaces have their own rules on loads, fixings and edges. See [terrace and podium play areas](/blog/terrace-and-podium-play-areas-for-high-rise-buildings.html).

## Example: a 12 m by 8 m courtyard

A courtyard of this size can hold a compact play station for 5 to 10-year-olds, two spring riders for toddlers, wall-mounted play panels along one side and a bench, with poured rubber across the safety zones and a hopscotch grid in the open area. Exact fit depends on the safe play area of each unit, so ask your supplier for a scaled layout.
