For a family with young children, the play area is one of the first amenities they look at on a site visit, and one they will use every day once they move in. That makes it one of the amenities with the most visible effect on how a project is perceived, at launch and at resale.

## Why play areas matter to buyers

- **Families are a large share of buyers.** Many choose between shortlisted projects partly on amenities.
- **It is visible.** Unlike plumbing or structure, a play area can be seen and judged in seconds.
- **It signals care.** A thoughtfully designed play area tells buyers the builder has thought about how people will live there.
- **It is used daily.** Residents value amenities they actually use, and recommend projects where children are happy.

## The catch: value depends on condition

A play area helps a project only if it still looks good years later. A set of faded plastic and rusting frames in a corner of the compound tells a visiting buyer that maintenance is poor. That is why material choices and a maintenance plan matter as much as the design.

## Choose for the long term

- **Materials:** galvanised and powder-coated steel, UV-stabilised roto-moulded plastic, or treated wood with stainless fixings. See [choosing durable playground materials](/blog/choosing-durable-playground-materials.html).
- **Design:** a recognisable structure, such as a tepee tower or nature-themed station, makes the play area part of the project's identity.
- **Handover:** give the society the maintenance guide, warranty documents and supplier contacts. A play area that is easy to look after stays attractive.

## Plan it early

Play areas fitted into leftover space after the layout is fixed are usually too small, poorly placed or in the shade of a tower all day. Plan the play area with the site layout, alongside landscaping and drainage.
