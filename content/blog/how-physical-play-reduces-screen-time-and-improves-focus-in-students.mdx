Screens are not going away, and they are not all bad. But when children spend most of their free time sitting with a phone or tablet, they miss the movement their bodies and minds need. Physical play is one of the best counterweights, and it helps in the classroom too.

## Why children choose screens

Often because they are the easiest, most interesting option available. A bare yard or a broken slide cannot compete. A play space with things to climb, spin, balance on and explore can.

## Play and concentration

Many teachers notice that students settle better after an active break. Moving gives the body a release after sitting, raises alertness and helps children return to quiet work ready to focus. Regular activity also helps children sleep better, which affects attention the next day.

## What schools can do

- **Active breaks:** short movement breaks between lessons.
- **A playground with variety** for all ages, so every child finds something. See [what every school playground should include](/blog/what-every-school-playground-should-include.html).
- **Outdoor lessons** in maths, science and language.
- **Device-free break times.**
- **Sports and clubs** beyond the timetable.

## What parents and societies can do

- Make outdoor play the default after school, with screens later.
- Keep a safe, interesting play area close to home. See [best play equipment for apartments](/blog/best-play-equipment-for-apartments-and-housing-complexes.html).
- Play with children sometimes; they copy what adults do.
- Agree family screen-time rules together.

## Easy active-break ideas for classrooms

- **Two-minute stretch** between lessons.
- **Walk-and-talk:** pairs discuss a question while walking around the corridor or playground.
- **Hop the answers:** children hop to cards on the floor to answer quiz questions.
- **Playground relay:** a short relay at the start of PE or after a long lesson.

## Making play the easy choice

Children choose what is easy and interesting. A play area that is close, safe and varied competes with screens; a bare yard does not.
