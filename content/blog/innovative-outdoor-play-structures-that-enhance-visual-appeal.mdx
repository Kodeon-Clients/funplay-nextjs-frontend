Most playground equipment is designed to be played on. Some is designed to be looked at too. In parks, resorts and townships, a striking play structure becomes a landmark: the thing people photograph, give directions by and remember.

## Ideas that stand out

### Tepee and tower forms

Tall, slender towers with nets and slides catch the eye from across a lawn. Our NexLev Pinnacle tepee towers were designed with this in mind.

### Sculptural and abstract

Structures that read as sculpture, with bold geometry and a restrained palette, suit contemporary landscapes. See [NexLev Abstract](/nexlev-series/abstract.html).

### Nature-inspired

Flowers, leaves, trees and animals in structural form. They look at home in gardens and connect children to nature.

### Origami and paper forms

Our [Kagzi](/funplay-originals/kagzi.html) range takes its shapes from folded paper: boats, planes, birds and frogs.

### Concept structures

Play built around an idea: a strange city, a musical garden, a junk park. See [conceptual designs](/funplay-originals/conceptual.html).

## Looks and play together

A landmark structure must still be busy. Check play value: how many children it serves, how many activities, which ages. The best designs are both.

## Placement

- Put it on a main view line: from the entrance, the clubhouse or the main path.
- Give it space to be seen; do not crowd it with other equipment.
- Light it for evening.

## Where landmark structures work best

- **Township entrances and central greens,** where a strong form helps people find their way and gives the project an identity.
- **Resorts,** where a distinctive play structure becomes part of the guest experience and the photographs families share. See [kids' play areas for resorts and hotels](/blog/kids-play-area-for-resorts-and-hotels.html).
- **Public parks,** where a recognisable structure becomes a meeting point for the neighbourhood.
- **Schools,** where a signature structure can become part of the school's identity.

## Balancing looks with practicality

Striking structures sometimes tempt designers towards extra height, unusual materials or very dark colours. Each has a cost: taller structures need larger safety zones and thicker surfacing, unusual materials may be harder to maintain, and dark colours get hot in Indian summers. A good supplier will explain these trade-offs early, so the final design is both beautiful and practical. See [playground safety standards in India](/blog/playground-safety-standards-in-india.html).
