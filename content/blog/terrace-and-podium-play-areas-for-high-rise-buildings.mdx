In Mumbai, Bengaluru, Pune and other dense cities, many housing projects have no ground-level open space left for a play area. It goes on the podium above the parking, or on a terrace. That is perfectly workable, but it needs more coordination than a garden playground.

## Start with the structure

Before choosing equipment, ask the structural consultant to confirm:

- **Load capacity** of the slab where the play area will go, including equipment, surfacing, people and any planters.
- **Approved fixing points** or zones where anchors can go.
- **Waterproofing system** and how it can be protected.
- **Drainage** falls and outlets.

## Fixing equipment without digging

On ground level, posts are set in concrete footings. On a slab, equipment is usually fixed with **base plates** anchored to the structural slab at approved points, or onto a reinforced plinth, coordinated with the waterproofing layers. Your supplier should provide base-plate details and loads for each item.

## Surfacing on a slab

The surface must protect children from falls and protect the waterproofing:

- Rubber tiles or poured rubber on a suitable base, with drainage beneath.
- Turf with a shock pad, laid to fall towards outlets.
- Never anchor through the waterproofing without the waterproofing contractor's detail.

See [fall height and safety surfacing](/blog/playground-fall-height-and-safety-surfacing-guide.html).

## Edges, wind and heat

- **Edges:** secure, unclimbable railings or walls, with no furniture or equipment close enough to climb on.
- **Wind:** tall structures and shade sails catch wind; ask about wind loads.
- **Heat:** terraces get hot. Use lighter colours, shade structures and avoid dark surfaces.

## Equipment that suits elevated spaces

- Compact multi play stations that stack activities vertically.
- Low climbers, spring riders and balance elements.
- Wall-mounted play panels along parapets and walls.
- Small rotating climbers with a small footprint.
