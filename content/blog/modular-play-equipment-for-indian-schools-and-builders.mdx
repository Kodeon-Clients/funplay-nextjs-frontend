Schools and builders rarely have a perfect, empty rectangle for a playground. The plot is narrow, or L-shaped, or on a podium, and the budget arrives in instalments. Modular play equipment is designed for exactly that situation.

## What makes equipment modular

Modular structures are built from a kit of standard parts:

- **Towers and decks** at set heights.
- **Access:** steps, ladders, climbing walls, nets.
- **Links:** bridges, tunnels, rope or wobbly bridges.
- **Exits:** slides of different shapes and sizes, poles.
- **Activity:** roofs, play panels, steering wheels, telescopes.

The same parts can be combined into a compact unit for a small courtyard or a long, multi-tower structure for a large lawn.

## Why schools and builders choose modular

- **Fits the site.** Towers and links can follow the shape of the plot.
- **Fits the users.** Deck heights and slides are chosen for the age group.
- **Fits the budget.** Start with fewer towers and add more later.
- **Faster to supply.** Standard parts are quicker to manufacture than one-off designs.
- **Easier to maintain.** Standard spares, standard fixings.

## Maintenance advantages

Because modular structures use standard parts, a damaged slide, panel or step can usually be replaced on its own, without replacing the whole structure. Keep a record of the range and colours installed so spares match.
