For generations, Indian classrooms have leaned on rote learning. The National Education Policy 2020 marked a shift, emphasising play-based and activity-based learning, especially in the foundational years from 3 to 8. Here is what that means and how schools can make it real.

## What play-based learning is

Children learn through play: building, pretending, exploring, moving, solving. Teachers guide it with materials, questions and goals. It ranges from free play, where children lead, to guided play, where the teacher shapes the activity towards a concept.

## Why it works

- Children learn concepts by doing, not only by hearing.
- Language grows through talk during play.
- Social skills develop in group games.
- Motivation stays high when learning is engaging.
- Movement supports attention and memory.

## In the classroom and outside

- **Classroom:** blocks, puzzles, role-play corners, games.
- **Playground:** counting and shape panels, themed structures for role play, sand and water, balance trails that turn into number lines, and games painted into the floor.

## How schools can start

1. Train teachers in guided play.
2. Set aside time in the timetable.
3. Use hands-on materials in every subject.
4. Take lessons outdoors.
5. Design play spaces with learning in mind. See [educational benefits of outdoor play in schools](/blog/educational-benefits-of-outdoor-play-in-schools.html).

## Examples of guided play

- **Number trail:** children hop along numbered posts or a painted track, adding as they go.
- **Shop in the play house:** children buy and sell with play money, practising counting and language.
- **Sand measuring:** filling and comparing containers introduces volume and estimation.
- **Shape hunt:** children find circles, triangles and squares in the play equipment.
- **Story walk:** a teacher tells a story that moves around the playground, and children act it out.

Each activity has a learning goal, but children experience it as play.

## Getting parents on board

Many parents in India worry that play means less learning. Schools can help by:

- Explaining the approach at orientation, with examples.
- Sharing photos and short notes on what children learned through play.
- Inviting parents to a play-based session.
- Pointing to NEP 2020, which places play-based learning at the heart of the foundational stage.

## Starting small

Schools do not need to change everything at once. Start with one class, one outdoor activity a week and one improvement to the playground, then build on what works.
