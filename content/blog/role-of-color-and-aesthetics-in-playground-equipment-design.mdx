Walk past a playground and colour is the first thing you notice. It is also one of the cheapest design choices to get right, and it affects far more than appearance.

## How children respond to colour

Bright, contrasting colours attract young children and help them read the space: this is a slide, this is a step, this is where I go. Older children respond to bolder forms and more sophisticated palettes. Too many colours together can look chaotic; a planned palette looks deliberate.

## Colour for safety and access

- Contrasting colours mark steps, edges and changes in level.
- A distinct colour around swing paths keeps children out.
- Strong contrast helps children with low vision find their way.

## Indian conditions

- **Heat:** dark colours absorb more heat; keep slides and surfaces in full sun lighter.
- **Fading:** UV-stabilised materials fade slowly; mid-tones hide it best.
- **Dust:** very pale colours show dirt quickly.

## Aesthetics beyond colour

Form, proportion, materials and how the play area sits in its landscape matter as much as colour. See [innovative outdoor play structures that enhance visual appeal](/blog/innovative-outdoor-play-structures-that-enhance-visual-appeal.html).

## Colour choices by age

- **Toddlers:** bright, cheerful, high-contrast colours help them read steps, edges and handholds.
- **Primary children:** bold colours and strong themes, such as castles, jungles and robots, fuel imaginative play.
- **Older children:** they often prefer more grown-up palettes and bold forms, such as tepee towers in earthy reds and blacks.

## How aesthetics affect use

A play area that looks cared for invites families in; one that looks faded and mismatched suggests neglect. Good aesthetics also help a play area fit its setting, which matters in premium residential projects, resorts and heritage parks. See [outdoor play equipment ideas to match your landscape design](/blog/outdoor-play-equipment-ideas-to-match-your-landscape-design.html).

## Tips for choosing colours

1. Start from the setting: buildings, paving and planting.
2. Pick two or three main colours and a neutral.
3. Use contrast where it helps safety: steps, edges, entrances.
4. Keep slides and surfaces in full sun lighter.
5. View real samples on site before deciding.
