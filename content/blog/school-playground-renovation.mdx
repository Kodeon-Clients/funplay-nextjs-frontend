Most schools are not building a playground from scratch. They have one already: equipment installed ten or fifteen years ago, some of it rusting, a slide nobody uses, concrete or bare soil underneath. Renovating well is usually better value than replacing everything.

## Step 1: Inspect what you have

Ask a supplier or trained inspector to assess each item and give you a written list:

- Is it structurally sound? Check the base of posts, welds and footings.
- Is the plastic cracked, brittle or badly faded?
- Are there design hazards: head-entrapment gaps, high platforms without barriers, sharp edges?
- Can spares be found?
- Is the surface underneath safe for its fall height?

## Step 3: Fix the surface

In many old school playgrounds, the biggest safety problem is the ground: concrete, paver blocks or compacted soil. Replacing it with rubber, turf over a shock pad or deep sand under all equipment is often the most important upgrade you can make. See [school playground surface materials](/blog/school-playground-surface-materials-which-one-is-best.html).

## Step 4: Fill the gaps

Once you know what stays, look at what is missing: a pre-primary zone, swings, ground-level play, shade, seating. See [what every school playground should include](/blog/what-every-school-playground-should-include.html).

## Step 5: Phase around the school year

- Do the main civil and surfacing work in the long summer holidays, before the monsoon.
- Keep part of the playground open if work runs into term, fenced from the work area.
- Add smaller items in shorter breaks.

## Step 6: Set up maintenance

A renovation is a good moment to start a proper routine: daily and monthly checks and a yearly inspection. Our [playground safety inspection checklist](/blog/playground-safety-inspection-checklist.html) is ready to use.
