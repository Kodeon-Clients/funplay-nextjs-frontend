Watch a mixed playground for ten minutes and you will see the problem: a toddler stands at the bottom of a slide while a nine-year-old comes down fast, or a group of older children takes over the little ones' play house. Age zones solve this by giving each group its own space and equipment.

## How to separate zones

- **Paths** between zones that children naturally follow.
- **Low planting** or hedges.
- **Change in surface colour** so the boundary is obvious.
- **Distance:** put the toddler zone furthest from swings and fast equipment.
- **A low fence** around the toddler area in busy parks.

## Where to put each zone

- **Toddler zone:** near the entrance and seating, so carers can sit close by; away from swings.
- **Younger children:** around the main structure, central and visible.
- **Older children:** at the far side, with space for faster play.
- **Fitness:** at the edge, near walking paths.

## Common mistakes

- One large structure for all ages, with a toddler slide next to a 7 ft drop.
- Toddler swings in the same bay as big-kid swings.
- Seating for parents facing away from the toddler zone.
- No zone at all for teenagers, who then use the children's equipment.

## Zones in a small space

Even a small society or school play area can separate ages: a toddler corner with a low unit and spring riders on one side, a compact play station for older children on the other, with a bench and planting between them.
