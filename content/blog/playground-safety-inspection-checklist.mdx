Playground equipment is built to last for years outdoors, but it is not maintenance-free. Monsoon rain, summer heat, coastal air and hundreds of children a day all take their toll. The difference between a playground that is still safe after ten years and one that is closed after three is usually a simple, regular check.

This checklist is written for the person who actually looks after the play area: a school's estate staff, a society's facility manager, a park caretaker. Print it, adapt it to your equipment, and keep a log.

## Three levels of inspection

The European standard EN 1176-7, which many Indian suppliers follow, describes three levels of inspection. They translate well into a routine for Indian sites:

## Daily check: a five-minute walk around

Do this before children arrive or before the play area opens in the evening.

## Monthly check: hands on every unit

The monthly check goes further. Bring a spanner set, a torch and a notebook or phone to record what you find.

<Checklist title="Monthly operational check" items={["Shake every unit: posts should not move in their footings","Check bolts and fixings you can reach; tighten anything loose and replace missing bolt caps","Swing chains, shackles and hangers: look for wear at the contact points, open links and worn bushes","Moving equipment (merry-go-rounds, see-saws, spinners, gym machines): listen for grinding, check for play in bearings, check stoppers and handles","Plastic parts: cracks, splits and heavy fading, especially on slide beds and roofs","Steel parts: rust at the base of posts, at welds and where paint is chipped","Surfacing: depth of sand or loose fill under swings and slide exits, gaps or lifted edges in rubber tiles, wear in turf","Edges and corners: no sharp edges or protruding bolt ends","Signs: rules board readable and contact number up to date"]} />

<Tip title="Where to look first" text="On Indian sites the same few problems come up again and again: worn swing hangers, loose bolts on platforms, rust where posts meet the ground, and sand or loose fill scattered away from swing and slide exits. Check these first every month." />

## Annual inspection: a full review

Once a year, ask your supplier or a trained inspector for a full inspection. It should cover everything in the monthly check plus:

- **Structural soundness:** foundations, welds, wall thickness of posts where corrosion has started, and the condition of wooden parts.
- **Wear of moving parts:** bearings, axles and swing hangers measured against the manufacturer's limits.
- **Compliance:** fall heights, safety zones, entrapment gaps and guard rail heights, checked against the standard the equipment was designed to. Changes made since installation, such as a new bench placed inside a swing's safety zone, are a common finding.
- **Surfacing performance:** whether the surface still absorbs falls from the height of each unit.
- **A written report** listing every defect, its risk level and the recommended action.

## What to do when you find a problem

Sort problems by how badly someone could be hurt:

1. **Could cause a serious injury** (a broken chain link, a loose platform, a sharp protruding edge, missing surfacing under a high unit): close the item the same day with tape or a barrier and a sign, record it, and call the supplier.
2. **Could cause a minor injury or will get worse** (a loose bolt cap, light rust, a small crack in a non-structural panel): log it and fix it within days.
3. **Cosmetic** (faded paint, a scratched panel): plan it into the next maintenance visit.

Always use the manufacturer's spare parts. Improvised repairs with wire, rope or the wrong size of bolt create new hazards.

## Keep a simple log

A notebook or a shared spreadsheet is enough. For each check, record the date, who checked, what was found and what was done. Over a year the log shows which items need attention most often and helps you plan spares and budgets. It also shows, if there is ever an accident, that the play area was looked after properly.
