Look at quotes from different playground suppliers in India and you will see two kinds of "plastic" for slides, tunnels and panels: roto-moulded polyethylene and FRP (fibre reinforced plastic). They look similar in photos. They behave quite differently over years of sun, rain and children.

## How each is made

**Roto-moulded polyethylene.** Plastic powder, usually linear low-density polyethylene (LLDPE) with UV stabilisers, is placed in a metal mould that is heated while it rotates on two axes. The melting plastic coats the inside of the mould evenly, forming a seamless hollow part with rounded edges. This is how we make our slides, tunnels, roofs and many panels.

**FRP (fibreglass).** Layers of glass fibre are laid in a mould with resin, usually behind a coloured gelcoat surface. It can be laid by hand, which makes one-off shapes economical.

## Which to choose

For slides, tunnels and panels in schools, societies and parks, we recommend UV-stabilised roto-moulded polyethylene. It is tough, forgiving when children knock or kick it, and safe even when damaged. FRP can make sense for large decorative or sculptural shapes that would need very large moulds.

## Looking after plastic parts

Wash with water and mild soap; avoid solvents. Check slide beds and joints for cracks at every monthly inspection. In summer, touch slides before small children use them in the afternoon.
