Ask ten people what makes a playground safe and you will get ten answers: soft flooring, rounded edges, supervision, good-quality steel. All of them are part of it. Safety standards pull these ideas together into measurements that a designer can draw and an inspector can check.

This guide explains the standards most commonly used in India, the handful of numbers that matter most, and what you should ask a supplier before you sign a purchase order.

## What is playground safety?

Playground safety does not mean removing all risk. Children need to climb, balance, swing and fall over now and then; that is how they learn what their bodies can do. The aim is to remove hazards children cannot see or judge for themselves: a hard surface under a high platform, a gap that can trap a head, a sharp bolt end, a swing placed in a walkway.

A safe playground comes from four things working together:

1. **Equipment designed** to a recognised standard.
2. **A surface** that absorbs falls from the height of that equipment.
3. **A layout** with clear safety zones and sensible traffic routes.
4. **Installation and maintenance** that keep it all as designed.

## The standards used in India

Most playground suppliers and buyers in India work to the European standards:

- **EN 1176** covers playground equipment: general safety requirements in part 1, then separate parts for swings, slides, cableways, carousels, rocking equipment and so on. Part 7 covers installation, inspection and maintenance.
- **EN 1177** covers impact-absorbing playground surfacing and how its critical fall height is tested.

Some tenders, especially for international schools and projects designed by foreign consultants, refer to the American ASTM standards instead. If your tender or authority specifies a standard, use that one and ask the supplier to confirm compliance in writing. If nothing is specified, EN 1176 and EN 1177 are a sensible reference.

## The numbers that matter most

### Free fall height

This is the height a child could fall from: usually the platform floor, or the highest point a child can stand or climb to. It decides how much protection the surface needs and how large the safety zone must be. Under EN 1176 the free fall height of equipment is limited to 3 metres.

### Critical fall height of the surface

Every tested surface has a critical fall height: the highest fall it can absorb without a high risk of serious head injury. **The surface's critical fall height must be at least the free fall height of the equipment above it.** EN 1176-1 also lists simpler surfaces that are acceptable up to certain heights, commonly summarised like this:

### The safety zone

Every piece of equipment needs clear space around it where a child might land or move: the impact area. For most equipment it extends at least 1.5 metres from the equipment and grows with the fall height. Swings need more, because the seat moves well beyond the frame. Safety zones of two items may overlap only in some cases, never for swings or the exits of slides.

### Entrapment gaps

Openings that are larger than a child's body but smaller than the head can trap a child by the neck. Standards test this with probes; gaps between roughly 89 mm and 230 mm are the ones designers avoid in reachable places. Small holes and slots that could trap a finger, roughly 8 to 25 mm, are avoided too, as are V-shaped gaps that can catch clothing or a drawstring.

### Guard rails and barriers

Platforms above a certain height need handrails or full barriers that children cannot easily climb over. The required height depends on the fall height and on the age group the equipment is designed for.

## Safety is decided on site, not in the brochure

A certificate tells you the design passed a test. It does not tell you whether the equipment will be safe in your playground. That depends on:

- **Layout.** Swings placed in the main walkway, slide exits facing a wall, or two items squeezed into one safety zone will cause injuries however well each one is made.
- **Surface installation.** Rubber tiles laid on an uneven base, or sand that has been scattered down to 50 mm, no longer protect anyone.
- **Foundations.** Posts must be set to the depth and concrete specification the manufacturer gives. A unit that wobbles is a unit that will fail.
- **Age group.** Equipment designed for 6 to 12-year-olds is not safe for toddlers, who cannot judge heights and gaps.
- **Maintenance.** A loose bolt or a worn swing chain turns a safe unit into an unsafe one. See our [playground safety inspection checklist](/blog/playground-safety-inspection-checklist.html).
