By about seven or eight, many children find a standard play station too easy. They start climbing the outside of the roof or going up the slide the wrong way, looking for the challenge the playground does not give them. Adventure equipment gives them that challenge in a designed, safer way.

## Why challenge matters

- **Strength and grip** from climbing and hanging.
- **Balance and coordination** from bridges and beams.
- **Risk judgement:** children learn to assess what they can do.
- **Confidence** from mastering something difficult.
- **Teamwork** on equipment that needs cooperation.

## Adventure equipment

- **Climbing nets and tepee towers** with several routes up.
- **Climbing walls** with holds of different difficulty.
- **Rope and wobbly bridges** between towers.
- **Rotating net climbers** that move as children climb.
- **Balance courses** of beams, posts and swinging steps.
- **Monkey bars and ring traverses.**

## Grade the challenge

Offer easy, medium and hard routes on the same structure: a ladder and a net, a low and a high bridge. Each child pushes their own limit without being forced into the hardest option.

## Manage hazards, not all risk

Remove the dangers children cannot see; keep the challenge they can judge.

- Correct fall heights and impact-absorbing surfaces.
- No entrapment gaps or sharp edges.
- Sound structures and fixings.
- Clear safety zones.

## Where adventure playgrounds fit

- **Schools,** where older primary children need more than a standard play station. Separate the adventure zone from the pre-primary area.
- **Parks,** where teenagers otherwise have nothing to do except misuse children's equipment.
- **Townships and resorts,** where a tall net tower doubles as a landmark.

## Talking to parents about risk

Parents sometimes worry when they see tall nets. Explain how the equipment is designed: fall heights within the standard, deep impact-absorbing surfaces, nets that catch rather than drop, and safety zones kept clear. Children on well-designed adventure equipment learn to judge their own limits, which serves them well beyond the playground.

## Maintenance for adventure equipment

Nets, ropes and moving parts need regular checks for wear, tension and connector condition. Include them in your monthly routine. See our [playground safety inspection checklist](/blog/playground-safety-inspection-checklist.html).
