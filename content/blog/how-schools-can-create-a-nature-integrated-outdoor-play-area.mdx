Many school campuses have a corner that is neither playground nor garden: a strip of soil, a few trees, an unused lawn. With some planning, it can become a nature play area that children love at break time and teachers use for lessons.

## Plan with teachers

Ask teachers how they would use an outdoor space: observing plants for science, measuring for maths, storytelling, art. Their ideas shape what goes in.

## Elements to include

- **Planting:** native, non-toxic, thornless plants; a small vegetable or herb garden.
- **Sensory path:** a barefoot trail of different textures: pebbles, sand, wood, grass.
- **Logs and stumps:** balance trails and seating.
- **Sand area** for digging and building.
- **Outdoor seating circle** for storytelling and lessons.
- **A few play structures,** ideally nature-inspired, for climbing and sliding.

## Keep it safe and usable

- Impact-absorbing surfaces under climbing equipment.
- Firm paths so the space is usable after rain.
- Shade from trees and sails.
- Clear sight lines for supervision.

## Look after it together

Assign responsibility: a teacher in charge, estate staff for heavy work and student "garden monitors" for watering and tidying. Children look after spaces they help create.

## Learning activities through the year

- **Monsoon:** observe rain, puddles, insects and plant growth; measure rainfall.
- **Winter:** plant vegetables and flowers; track growth.
- **Summer:** study shade and temperature; water plants; plan shade-friendly play times.

## Common pitfalls

- **No maintenance plan.** Gardens and sand areas decline quickly without care.
- **Thorny or toxic plants** in reach of children.
- **No impact surfaces** under climbing elements because "it's a nature area". Safety rules still apply.
- **Waterlogging.** Plan drainage before planting.
