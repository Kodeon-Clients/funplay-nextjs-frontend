Almost everyone agrees Indian children need more places to play. Yet new playgrounds are slow to appear and many existing ones are broken. The obstacles are practical, and most have practical answers.

## 1. Land

Open land in cities is scarce and valuable. Play areas lose out to parking, buildings and roads.

**What helps:** podium and terrace play areas, pocket parks on small plots, school grounds shared with the neighbourhood after hours, and play elements along streets and plazas. See [terrace and podium play areas](/blog/terrace-and-podium-play-areas-for-high-rise-buildings.html).

## 2. Heat

Unshaded equipment is unusable on summer afternoons.

**What helps:** trees, shade sails, roofed structures and lighter colours. See [playground shade ideas](/blog/playground-shade-ideas-for-indian-summers.html).

## 3. Monsoon

Waterlogged sites, slippery surfaces, rust.

**What helps:** drainage designed in, galvanised steel, rubber surfaces on drained bases. See [monsoon maintenance](/blog/how-to-maintain-playground-equipment-in-monsoon.html).

## 4. Maintenance

The most common reason playgrounds fail is that nobody owns their upkeep after opening.

**What helps:** a named agency or person, a budget, an inspection routine and an AMC. See our [playground safety inspection checklist](/blog/playground-safety-inspection-checklist.html).

## 5. Vandalism and misuse

Adults on children's equipment, theft of parts, damage at night.

**What helps:** robust, tamper-resistant equipment, lighting, a sense of ownership among residents, and outdoor gyms so adults have their own equipment.

## 6. Procurement

Tenders that buy the cheapest equipment which then fails early.

**What helps:** specifications based on standards and materials, and evaluation on quality and life-cycle cost. See [how to write a tender specification](/blog/playground-equipment-tender-specification.html).

## 7. Access

Playgrounds that are far away, unsafe to reach or not usable by children with disabilities.

**What helps:** smaller play areas closer to homes, safe routes and inclusive design.

## Learning from what works

Play areas that last in Indian cities tend to share a few traits: they are close to homes, overlooked, shaded, built with robust equipment, and looked after by someone who feels responsible for them. None of these require a huge budget, but all of them require planning.
