Give a child a slide and they will slide. Give them a log, a mound and a patch of sand, and they will invent a dozen games. Natural elements invite open-ended play, and combined with equipment they make a play space feel like a place to explore.

## Natural elements that work

- **Logs and stumps** for balancing, stepping and sitting.
- **Boulders** for low climbing and scrambling.
- **Mounds** for running up, rolling down and carrying slides.
- **Sand** for digging and building.
- **Water** channels and pumps, where they can be managed.
- **Planting** for hiding, paths and sensory variety.

## Combining with equipment

- A play tower on a mound, with a slide down the slope.
- A trail of logs and stepping posts linking two structures.
- A sand area beside a play house, so pretend games spill over.
- Nature-inspired structures, such as tree houses and leaf canopies, that look as if they grew there.

## Keep it safe

- Logs and boulders must be stable and set firmly.
- Smooth surfaces, no splinters or sharp edges.
- Impact-absorbing surfaces where children can fall from a height.
- Clean, washed sand, covered when not in use if animals are around.
- Water features designed to drain fully and stay clean.

## Maintenance

Natural elements need more care than steel and plastic: rake sand, check logs for rot, trim planting, clear water channels. Build this into the maintenance plan from day one.

## Combining natural and built elements

The best results come from mixing: a nature-inspired play structure as the anchor, natural elements around it for open-ended play, and planting to tie it into the landscape. Structures from our [Organic Play](/funplay-originals/organic-play.html) and [Nature Sparsh](/funplay-originals/nature-sparsh.html) ranges were designed to sit in exactly this kind of setting.

## Involving children

Children are experts in natural play. Ask them what they would like: a place to dig, to hide, to balance, to collect things. Their answers often shape the most popular parts of the space.
