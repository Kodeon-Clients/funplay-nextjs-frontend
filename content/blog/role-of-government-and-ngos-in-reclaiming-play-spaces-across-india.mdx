Reclaiming play space in India's cities and villages is not a job any one group can do alone. Government, NGOs, companies and communities each hold a piece of the solution.

## Government and municipal bodies

They control most public land and budgets, so they can:

- Protect parks and open land in city plans.
- Add play to parks, streets, plazas and redevelopment projects.
- Fund equipment, surfacing and, crucially, maintenance.
- Procure through clear tenders. See [how to write a tender specification](/blog/playground-equipment-tender-specification.html).

## NGOs and community groups

They work closest to children and residents, so they can:

- Identify unused spaces and local needs.
- Involve children in designing their spaces.
- Organise play streets and community events.
- Help residents take ownership of a space and keep it cared for.

## Communities and residents

Residents' associations and parents can look after local play spaces, report damage and push for improvements.

## Maintenance: the missing piece

Many projects fail after opening because nobody owns their upkeep. Every plan, whoever funds it, needs a named agency, a budget and a routine. See our [inspection checklist](/blog/playground-safety-inspection-checklist.html).

## Steps for a successful project

1. **Identify the space** with residents and the authority: unused plots, neglected parks, school grounds.
2. **Involve children** in deciding what goes in. Drawings, votes and site walks work well.
3. **Agree who owns maintenance** before anything is installed, with a budget.
4. **Design for durability and inclusion,** with a layout, safety zones and surfacing. See [planning a public park playground](/blog/planning-a-public-park-playground.html).
5. **Open the space with the community,** and post rules and a contact for reporting damage.
6. **Review after a year:** what is used, what is broken, what is missing.

## Why maintenance decides success

Many well-meant play spaces in India are opened with a ceremony and then slowly fall apart. Equipment breaks, nobody repairs it, the space is closed and the land is used for something else. Funding maintenance for at least the first few years, and naming who is responsible, is what turns a project into a lasting play space.
