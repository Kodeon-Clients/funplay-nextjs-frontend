The play area is one of the amenities buyers see first and residents use most. For a builder, it is also one of the few amenities that can be planned, installed and shown relatively quickly. Done well, it supports sales and the project's reputation for years. Done as an afterthought, it ends up in the shade of a tower, too small for the families who move in.

## 1. Plan it at the design stage

Bring the playground supplier in when the landscape architect is working on the site plan. At that stage you can still:

- Give the play area a central, visible location with sunlight.
- Avoid podium structural elements, ramps, services and drainage lines.
- Plan levels and drainage for the safety surface.
- Reserve space for future phases.

## 2. Size it for the families, not the leftover space

Estimate how many children will live in the project and how many will play at peak time. A project of a few hundred flats needs a proper play zone with separate toddler and older-children areas, not two swings. Remember that every unit needs its safety zone; see [playground layout and safety zones](/blog/playground-layout-and-safety-zones.html).

## 4. Podium and terrace sites

Many city projects put the play area on a podium. Check slab load, waterproofing and drainage with the structural consultant, and use base-plate fixings designed for podiums. See [terrace and podium play areas](/blog/terrace-and-podium-play-areas-for-high-rise-buildings.html).

## 5. Time the installation

Install after heavy construction traffic around the area has finished, so the equipment and surface are not damaged, but early enough to show buyers and to be ready at possession. Coordinate surfacing with the landscape contractor.
