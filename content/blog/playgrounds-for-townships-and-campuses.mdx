A township of several thousand homes, a university campus or a railway colony is really a small town. One play area is not enough. Children need somewhere to play close to home, and the community needs a bigger shared space for weekends and events.

## Think in a network, not a single playground

- **Neighbourhood play areas** near each cluster of homes, within a short walk, for younger children and everyday play.
- **A central play space** with larger equipment, an outdoor gym, seating and open ground, for older children, families and events.
- **Play along the way:** a few pieces near walking routes, schools or community centres.

## Plan by walking distance

Parents with toddlers will not walk far. Older children range more widely. Map where families live and place smaller play areas so every home has one within easy reach.

## Serve every age

- Toddler areas with low equipment.
- Multi play stations and climbers for 5 to 12-year-olds.
- Climbing nets, adventure structures and sports courts for teenagers.
- Outdoor gyms and walking paths for adults and older residents. See [how to plan an outdoor gym](/blog/how-to-plan-an-outdoor-gym-in-a-park.html).

## Institutional requirements

Campuses run by railways, PSUs, universities and large companies often procure through tenders and need clear documentation: specifications, compliance evidence, warranties and maintenance plans. See [how to write a playground equipment tender specification](/blog/playground-equipment-tender-specification.html).

## Durability and upkeep

Spread over a large site, play areas are harder to supervise and maintain. Choose robust commercial equipment, standardise ranges so spares are common across sites, and set up a schedule with a named team. See our [playground safety inspection checklist](/blog/playground-safety-inspection-checklist.html).

## Phasing with construction

Large townships are built in phases. Plan the full play network on the master plan, then install play areas as each phase is handed over, so early residents are not left without play space.
