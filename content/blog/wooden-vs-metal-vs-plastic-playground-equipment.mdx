Should the play area be timber, steel or bright plastic? It is often the first design question and it affects the look of the whole site. In practice almost all modern equipment combines materials. The question is which one leads.

## Wood

Natural timber suits gardens, resorts and nature-themed schools. It feels warm to the touch and blends with planting.

**But in India:** termites, monsoon moisture and strong sun are hard on timber. It needs the right species, pressure treatment, stainless fixings, regular inspection for splits and splinters, and periodic re-treatment.

## Metal

Steel is the frame of almost all commercial equipment. It is strong, allows slim, open designs and handles heavy use.

**But:** it must be galvanised and powder coated to resist rust, and exposed metal surfaces get hot in the sun. See [galvanised vs powder-coated steel](/blog/galvanised-vs-powder-coated-steel-playground-equipment.html).

## Plastic

Roto-moulded polyethylene makes slides, tunnels, roofs and panels: colourful, seamless, smooth and low maintenance.

**But:** quality varies a lot. Thin or non-UV-stabilised plastic fades and turns brittle. See [roto-moulded plastic vs FRP](/blog/roto-moulded-plastic-vs-frp-play-equipment.html).

## How to choose

- **Green, landscaped or resort settings:** wood-finish or nature-inspired designs.
- **Schools and parks with heavy use:** steel frames with plastic slides and panels.
- **Toddler areas:** plastic for smooth, rounded, low-maintenance surfaces.
- **Themed areas:** whichever material best carries the theme.

## Maintenance compared

- **Solid wood:** inspect for splits, splinters and rot; re-treat periodically; check fixings.
- **Steel:** check for rust at post bases and welds; touch up coatings.
- **Plastic:** wash; check for cracks and fading.
- **Laminates:** wash; check edges and fixings.
