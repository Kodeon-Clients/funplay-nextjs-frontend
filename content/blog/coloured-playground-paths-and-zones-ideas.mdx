Colour in a playground is not only decoration. Used well in the floor, it organises the space, guides children, keeps them out of danger zones and adds extra play without adding equipment.

## Colour to organise

- **Zones by age:** one colour family for the toddler area, another for older children. Children and parents understand the difference without a fence.
- **Safety zones:** a distinct colour or ring around swings and slide exits tells children "don't walk here".
- **Paths:** a continuous coloured path shows the route between entrance, equipment and seating, and keeps children out of swing paths.

## Colour to play

Poured rubber can carry games built into the surface:

- Hopscotch and number grids.
- Alphabet and counting tracks for pre-primary children.
- Mazes and spirals.
- Racing lanes and start lines.
- Stepping-stone patterns and islands.
- Maps, rivers and roads for pretend play.

## Colour for everyone

High contrast helps children with low vision and makes edges, steps and changes in level easier to see. Use a strongly contrasting colour for path edges and around entrances.

## Practical tips for Indian sites

- **Heat:** very dark colours (black, navy, dark red) get hot in summer sun. Use lighter tones in sunny areas.
- **Fading:** UV-stable EPDM fades slowly; plan colours that still look good slightly softened.
- **Dirt:** mid-tones hide dust better than white or very pale colours.
- **Cost:** complex patterns take longer to lay; keep detail where it adds play value.
- **Safety first:** the surface must still have the right thickness for the fall height under equipment. See [fall height and safety surfacing](/blog/playground-fall-height-and-safety-surfacing-guide.html).

## Sample colour scheme for a school

- **Pre-primary zone:** soft greens and yellows, with a number track and stepping-stone pattern.
- **Primary zone:** blues and oranges, with a racing lane and maze.
- **Swing zone:** a contrasting red band marking the clear area.
- **Paths:** a neutral grey with a bright edge stripe.
