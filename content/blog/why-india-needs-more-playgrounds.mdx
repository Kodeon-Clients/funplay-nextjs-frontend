Every child needs somewhere safe to run, climb and play with friends. In much of India, that place is getting harder to find.

## The current situation

- **Cities:** open plots, colony grounds and lanes have given way to buildings, roads and parking. Remaining parks may be far away, crowded or poorly maintained.
- **Housing:** many older societies have no play area; newer ones sometimes have only a token corner.
- **Schools:** many schools, especially in dense areas, have little outdoor space or only bare ground.
- **Villages:** there is often open land, but little or no safe play equipment.

## Why it matters

Without safe places to play, children are less active, have fewer chances to make friends and test themselves, and may play on streets or in unsafe places. See [the benefits of outdoor play](/blog/unlocking-potential-through-outdoor-play.html).

## Who is most affected

- Children in low-income urban areas.
- Girls, whose movement is often limited to spaces close to home.
- Children with disabilities, who cannot use typical equipment. See [inclusive playgrounds](/blog/growing-need-for-inclusive-playgrounds-in-indian-communities.html).
- Children in villages and government schools.

## What can be done

- **Cities:** protect and create open space, add play to parks and streets, fund maintenance. See [from concrete to playgrounds](/blog/from-concrete-to-playgrounds-how-indian-cities-can-create-more-child-friendly-spaces.html).
- **Builders:** plan proper play areas in every project.
- **Schools:** improve playgrounds step by step.
- **Companies:** fund playgrounds through CSR in under-served areas.
- **Communities:** look after the spaces they have.

## What a good playground needs

More playgrounds only help if they are safe and last. Each one needs:

- Equipment designed for public use, built to a recognised safety standard.
- Impact-absorbing surfaces across all safety zones.
- A layout with something for each age group.
- Shade, seating and lighting.
- A named person or agency responsible for maintenance.

See [planning a public park playground](/blog/planning-a-public-park-playground.html) and [what every school playground should include](/blog/what-every-school-playground-should-include.html).

## Where to start

The quickest gains are often in places that already exist: a neglected park, a bare school ground, an unused corner of a housing society. Restoring and equipping these spaces is faster and cheaper than finding new land.
