Eco-friendly playground design is less about special products and more about a series of sensible choices. Together they make a play space that is cooler, greener, cheaper to run and closer to nature.

## Design features to consider

### Keep and add trees

Design around existing trees and plant new ones for shade.

### Nature-inspired structures

Equipment shaped like trees, leaves, flowers and animals, such as our [Nature Sparsh](/funplay-originals/nature-sparsh.html) and [Organic Play](/funplay-originals/organic-play.html) ranges, connects children to nature even in a city.

### Native planting

Hardy native plants need less water and attract birds and butterflies children can observe.

### Permeable paths and drained surfaces

Gravel, permeable paving and well-drained play surfaces let monsoon rain soak in.

### Rain gardens

Planted dips that collect run-off and turn it into a feature.

### Solar lighting

Solar path and area lights extend evening use without wiring or running costs.

### Natural play elements

Logs, boulders, sand and mounds add open-ended play with low-impact materials. See [using natural elements with play equipment](/blog/using-natural-elements-with-play-equipment-for-immersive-spaces.html).

### Durable, repairable equipment

Equipment that lasts and can be repaired with standard spares produces far less waste than cheap units replaced every few years.

## For builders and developers

Eco-friendly play areas suit projects that market green features. Plan the play area with the landscape architect from the start, keep mature trees, use permeable paving around the play area and choose durable, repairable equipment. Our guide to [playground amenities for real estate projects](/blog/playground-amenities-for-real-estate-projects.html) explains how to plan this.

## For schools and parks

Eco-friendly design can also be a teaching tool. A rain garden, a butterfly bed or a compost corner beside the playground gives teachers material for science lessons, and children learn to look after the space. See [how schools can create a nature-integrated outdoor play area](/blog/how-schools-can-create-a-nature-integrated-outdoor-play-area.html).

## A note on recycled materials

Recycled plastics and rubber can reduce environmental impact, but they must meet the same safety and durability requirements as new materials. A recycled part that cracks after two monsoons creates more waste, not less. Ask suppliers about both recycled content and expected life.
