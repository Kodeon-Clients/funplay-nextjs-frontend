Visit any park in an Indian city at 6 am and you will see who uses the open gym most: older residents on their morning walk. In housing societies it is the same, with grandparents exercising while grandchildren play nearby. Planning an outdoor gym with seniors in mind makes it more used, not less.

## What older users need

- **Low impact:** movements that are gentle on knees, hips and back.
- **Support:** handrails and handholds to steady themselves.
- **Easy access:** level ground, low step-on heights, no stepping over bars.
- **Rest:** seating between stations.
- **Comfort:** shade and drinking water.
- **Confidence:** clear signs showing how to use each machine.

## Layout details that matter

- Place the gym **next to the walking path** people already use.
- Keep it **step-free and level**, with a firm, non-slip surface.
- Put **benches between stations** so users can rest.
- Plan **shade** for early-morning and evening sun.
- Add **lighting** for winter mornings and evenings.
- Use **signs in the local language** with pictures.

## Near the play area, but separate

Grandparents often bring children. Placing the gym near the play area lets them exercise and watch, but keep it separate so young children do not climb on gym machines.
