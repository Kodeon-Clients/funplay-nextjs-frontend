Most of the effort in planning a school playground goes into the equipment. The surface underneath often gets whatever budget is left. Yet the surface decides how badly a child is hurt when they fall, how the play area looks after its third monsoon, and how much work the estate team has every week.

This guide compares the surfaces Indian schools actually use, on the things a principal or trustee has to weigh: safety, weather, upkeep and cost.

## Why the surface matters in Indian schools

- **Safety.** Falls cause most playground injuries. The surface under a unit must absorb a fall from the highest point a child can reach on it. See [fall height and safety surfacing](/blog/playground-fall-height-and-safety-surfacing-guide.html) for how this is measured.
- **Monsoon.** A surface that holds water is closed for weeks every year and wears out faster.
- **Heat.** Dark surfaces in full sun get very hot between March and June.
- **Heavy use.** A school playground may see several hundred children a day. Grass and thin layers of sand do not survive that.
- **Upkeep.** Some surfaces need almost nothing; others need daily raking.

### 1. Rubber tiles

Rubber tiles are the most common choice for city schools. They are tested for the fall height they protect at each thickness, so you can lay thicker tiles under a tall play tower and thinner ones under a spring rider. Damaged tiles can be replaced one at a time. They need a firm, level base, usually concrete, with a slight slope so water runs off.

**Best for:** schools that want low upkeep and a tidy look, with equipment of all heights.

### 2. Poured rubber (EPDM)

A shock-absorbing base layer is poured on site and topped with coloured EPDM granules. Because it is seamless, it can carry hopscotch grids, running tracks, numbers and pathways, which suits pre-primary sections. Quality depends on the installer and the mix, so look at a two or three-year-old installation before choosing a contractor.

**Best for:** pre-primary and primary areas, and schools that want games and colours built into the floor.

### 3. Artificial turf with a shock pad

Turf stays green and clean all year, does not hold puddles and looks good next to a lawn. Under play equipment it must have a tested shock pad beneath it; turf laid directly on concrete or soil gives very little protection. In peak summer, turf in full sun gets warm, so plan shade or play times.

**Best for:** schools with lawns or nature-themed play areas, and mixed play and sports spaces.

### 4. Sand

Deep, clean sand is a genuinely safe surface and the cheapest one to install. It needs about 300 mm of depth plus extra for scattering, a border to contain it, a drained base and regular raking, cleaning and topping up. Use washed river sand, not construction sand with sharp grit and clay. In open campuses, fencing helps keep animals out.

**Best for:** schools on a tight budget with staff who can maintain it daily, and nature play areas where digging is part of the fun.

### 5. Grass

Grass is pleasant under low equipment and for open running space, but under heavy use it wears into bare, compacted soil within months. It is not a safe surface under slides, climbers or swings.

### Concrete and paver blocks

Hard surfaces have a place on paths, seating areas and around the edge of the play area. They should never sit inside the safety zone of play equipment.

## How to choose for your school

<Checklist title="Questions to settle before you choose" items={["What is the highest fall height of the equipment you plan to install?","Does the site flood or hold water in the monsoon? Where will water drain?","How many children will use the area at the same time, and for how many hours a day?","Who will maintain the surface, and how often can they do it?","Is the area in full sun? Can you add shade?","Do you want games, numbers or tracks built into the floor?"]} />

Most schools end up with a combination: rubber or turf with a shock pad under all the equipment, poured rubber with games for the pre-primary zone, and pavers or concrete for paths and seating. That puts the money where children fall.

<Tip title="Plan the drainage first" text="Before choosing a finish, walk the site after heavy rain. Where water collects is where the surface will fail first. A slight slope and a drain along the low edge add little to the cost and add years to the life of any surface." />
