Every FunPlay structure, from a spring rider to a multi-tower play station, goes through the same process. It starts with children and ends with a product we can make well and install safely.

## 1. Watching children play

We begin by observing children in real playgrounds, schools and parks: what they go back to, what bores them, how they invent games, where they get stuck. We talk to them too. Their answers are often more useful than any brief.

## 2. Ideas and sketches

The team brainstorms around what we saw, then sketches. Many ideas are dropped; the strongest are developed further. The aim is high play value: many ways to play, several senses, room for imagination.

## 3. Engineering in CAD

Concepts are modelled in CAD (we use SolidWorks). Engineers check them against safety requirements, including fall heights, gaps and clearances, and against our manufacturing processes: roto-moulding, fabrication and finishing.

## 4. Renderings and review

3D renderings show the look, colours and scale. The team reviews each design against its goals: play value, safety, durability, cost and appearance.

## 5. Prototype and production

Moulds and jigs are made, a first unit is produced and checked, and the design goes into our range. Many of our [FunPlay Originals](/funplay-originals.html) designs, including the origami-inspired [Kagzi](/funplay-originals/kagzi.html) range, came out of this process.

## Custom projects

For custom work, the same steps apply, starting with the site, the users and the client's goals. See [customization options for themed playgrounds](/blog/customization-options-for-themed-playgrounds.html).

## From range to custom

Most projects use designs from our ranges, sometimes adapted in colour or layout to fit the site. When a project needs something unique, such as a resort theme or a landmark for a township, the same process starts again with that site and its users. Custom work takes longer because of design development, approvals and sometimes new moulds, so we recommend raising it early.

## Learning from installed projects

We visit installed playgrounds to see how children actually use them: what gets crowded, what wears fastest, what is ignored. Those observations feed back into new designs and improvements to existing ones.
